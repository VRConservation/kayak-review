# Summary
I purchased this paddle in the US since it was about £100 and for some reason I had to have it. I then nervously carried it on flights, like a musician with their priceless Stradivarius, hoping that the attendants wouldn't see it or care that my carry on was too long. Finally returning to the UK, I went out for a paddle and was amazed with how light it was in hand and it narrow profile allowed paddling into the wind.

This is an expensive paddle and it is somewhat fragile. However, I've used it in many types of conditions and it has worn well. I've had not difficulties with the locking mechanism, but it does require some care, rinsing after every use and keeping dry to work well or it can become stiff.

Kalliste paddles have been criticized for a wobble. I've had this happen as well and it deeply concerned me when first purchased. However, as my paddle stroke has improved, the wobble disappeared. To be honest, you can wobble any blade if you don't have proper technique. The Kalliste loves a deep, upright canoe j-stroke and is very powerful when doing so, but it also works for a less upright stroke.

Although I very much like this paddle, it has often become the second, riding the bench on the foredeck or not being taken out at all. It is my go to to take for longer trips over several days simply because it's so light and good in the wind.

# Buyer
Expeditioner or a curator of fine lightweight paddles.
