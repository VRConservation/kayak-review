# Summary
I purchased this paddle in the US since it was about £100, and for some reason, I had to have it. I then nervously carried it on flights, like a musician with their priceless Stradivarius, hoping that the attendants wouldn't see it or care that my carry-on was too long. Finally returning to the UK, I went out for a paddle and was amazed by how light it was in hand, and its narrow profile allowed paddling into the wind.

This is an expensive paddle, and it is somewhat fragile. However, I've used it under many conditions, and it has worn well. I've had no difficulties with the locking mechanism, but it does require some care: rinse after every use and keep it dry to work well. It can become stiff.

Kalliste paddles have been criticized for a wobble. I've had this happen as well, and it deeply concerned me when it was first purchased. However, as my paddle stroke has improved, the wobble disappeared. To be honest, you can wobble any blade if you don't have proper technique. The Kalliste loves a deep, upright canoe J-stroke and is very powerful when doing so, but it also works for a less upright stroke.

Although I very much like this paddle, it has often become the second, riding the bench on the foredeck or not being taken out at all. It is my go to to take for longer trips over several days simply because it's so light and good in the wind.

# Buyer
Expeditioner or a curator of fine lightweight paddles.
