# Summary
:::{warning} Build quality

 **Although I've mended each paddle with carbon fibre tape and fiberglass epoxy, I can't really recommend this paddle after this issue**. See details below
:::

From the moment I tried out this paddle from a friend to the present, this has been my go-to paddle. I've used this paddle for short and long distances, from a few kilometers to over 70 km. It's a solid paddle that fits my hands and gives me confidence for forward strokes and ruddering. The Omega is lightweight and durable, even withstanding hits from rocks (though it has chipped in a few spots from paddling in places I shouldn't have). The leverlock is simple and easy to use. I've only had to tighten it once or twice, and the paddles are removable from the shaft, making travel or replacement easy. Another plus is the customization that lets you dial in the right paddle size, from blade to length.

I would put the price at medium for paddles. Paddles may seem pricey to beginners, but when you think about how much you use them, they're your engine, steering wheel, and brakes. It is about getting the paddle right and spending some simoleons on a good one. Word of warning tho, don't spend a lot until you try out a lot of different paddles. Just ask folks to borrow theirs for a few minutes on each trip and get a feel for what you like. 

## May 2026 update
Leaving for a paddle, I noticed two small cracks on both paddle blades. Both cracks appeared to have the same width and are in the same exact spot about mid-blade. I spoke to another paddler in the club who had the same issue with the same paddle. Celtic/NDK only said the blades were out of warranty and could offer a discount on new blade replacements. To me, this seems to be a serious manufacturer's defect, calling into question the blade's durability and reliability (or the massive force I apply to every paddle stroke!).
