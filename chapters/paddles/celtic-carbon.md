# Summary
From the moment I tried out this paddle from a friend to the present, this has been my go-to paddle. I've used this paddle all over for short and long distances, from paddles of a few kilometers to over 70 km. It's a solid paddle that fits right in my hands and gives confidence for forward strokes and ruddering. The Omega is lightweight and durable, even withstanding hits from rocks (though it has chipped in a few spots from paddling in places I shouldn't have with this paddle). The leverlock is simple and easy to use. I've only had to tighten it once or twice, and the paddles are removable from the shaft, making travel or replacement easy. One additional plus is the degree of customization that's possible to dial in the correct paddle size from blade to length.

I would put the price at medium for paddles. Paddles may seem pricey to beginners, but when you think about how much you use them, they're your engine, steering wheel, and brakes. It is getting the paddle right and spending some simoleons on a good one. Word of warning tho, don't spend a lot until you try out a lot of different paddles. Just ask folks to borrow theirs for a few minutes on each trip and get a feel for what you like. 

## May 2026 update
Leaving for a paddle, I noticed two small cracks on both paddle blades. Both cracks appeared to have the same width and are in the same exact spot about mid blade. I spoke to another paddler in the club that had the same issue with the same paddle. Celtic/NDK only said the blades were out of warranty and could offer a discount on new blade replacements. To me this seems to be a serious manufacturer's defect calling into question the blade's durability and reliability (or the massive force I apply to every paddle stroke!). **Although I've mended each paddle with carbon fibre tape and fiberglass epoxy, I can't really recommend this paddled following this issue**.
